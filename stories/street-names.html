Origin of Ottawa Street Names

What’s in a name? For Ottawa streets, their names honour politicians, local pioneers, local businessmen, royalty, significant Ottawa citizens and place names. They help to keep alive the significant parts of the history of the nation’s Capital. Although there are many ways Ottawa streets have been named, this video will look at a few select themes.

Pioneers and Settlers

In the early days of Bytown, streets were often named after prominent citizens in the young town. Names such as O’Connor Street, named after Daniel O’Connor who set up business during the construction of the Rideau Canal; Sparks and Nicholas Streets, both named after one of the area’s earliest settlers Nicholas Sparks; Slater Street, named after Sparks’ son-in-law and assistant engineer on improvement works for the Ottawa River, James Dyson Slater; Besserer Street, named after Louis Theodore Besserer who owned the land that is now Sandy Hill; Stewart Street, named after Dr. James Stewart who opened his practice in Bytown in 1827 and Lebreton Street, named after Captain John LeBreton, a naval officer who owned land near the Chaudiere Falls.

Further outside Bytown, some roads have been named after early farmers in Carleton County.

Eagleson Road was named after William Eagleson who owned a farm at the corner of Eagleson and Hazeldean Roads[1] while Robertson Road was named after John Robertson, an early settler near Bells Corners[2]. Smyth and Heron Roads were named after William Smyth and Gilbert Heron, some of the earliest settlers in Gloucester Township[3].

Local Entrepreneurs and Philanthropists

Various local businessmen have had streets named after them as well. Booth Street was named after John Rudolphus Booth, a lumber baron who got his start in Ottawa. Bronson Avenue was named after Erskine Henry Bronson, a local businessman of many trades, mainly lumber. MacLaren Street, named after another lumber baron, James MacLaren. Bruyère Street was named after Élisabeth Bruyère who helped establish the first hospital in Bytown along with the Sisters of Charity.

Politicians, Military and Royalty

Many politicians, local, provincial, national and even international, have had their names given to Ottawa streets. Fisher Avenue, Lewis, Waller and Friel Streets, to name a few, were all named after former mayors of Ottawa. Laurier Avenue and Mackenzie Avenue were named after Canadian prime ministers. Many streets were named after Governors General of Canada, such as Dalhousie and Elgin Streets, Vanier Parkway, Lisgar and many more.

Wellington Street was named after a British soldier, Arthur Wellesley, the 1st Duke of Wellington, who defeated Napoleon at the Battle of Waterloo and later became a British prime minister. Gladstone Avenue was named after British Prime Minister William Ewart Gladstone. Churchill Avenue was named after British Prime Minister Sir Winston Churchill after the 2nd World War.

As Bytown and Ottawa were part of the British Empire, many early streets were named after British military personnel and royalty. Queen Street was named in honour of the reigning monarch at the time Queen Victoria. Other streets named after royalty are Albert Street, son of Victoria and later King Edward VII, Clarence Street, 3rd son of George III and later William IV, Charlotte Street, only child of George IV.

Murray Street was named after General Sir James Murray, first Military Governor of Québec in 1759. Nelson Street was named after Horatio Nelson, Admiral of the British Fleet at the Battle of Trafalgar. And of course, Colonel By Drive was named after Colonel John By, head engineer and supervisor for the construction of the Rideau Canal.

Destinations

Roads in Ottawa were also named for their destinations. Russell Road, once an indigenous trail that led to Montreal, was named due to the fact that it was the road connecting Ottawa to the community of Russell Ontario. Montreal Road was once the main road from Ottawa to Montreal[3]. Richmond Road, one of the earliest roads built by European settlers in Ottawa, was the connection between the Ottawa River at Richmond Landing to the military village of Richmond.

Suburbs

In the suburbs of Ottawa, groups of streets often follow certain themes. The streets in the area known as Guildwood Estates in Alta Vista were named after US states such as Connecticut and Illinois[3]. In the Hunt Club area near Conroy Road, streets were named after Ottawa and other photographers, such as Karsh Drive and Topley Crescent[3]. In Bridlewood, streets were named based on equestrian words, names and breeds, such as Equestrian Drive and Steeple Chase Drive. The streets in Beaverbrook were named after Canadian artists and authors such as Carr Crescent and Leacock Drive. For an area of Orleans, the theme is French wines such as Bordeau Grove and Beaujolais Crescent[3].

Katimavik-Hazeldean has several themes: Canadian broadcasters and entertainers, arctic explorers and Inuit people and groups and Canadian inventors.
