Ottawa Shorts Ban

In the summer of 1950, there was a significant drive in Ottawa to ban shorts in public.

On June 13, 1950, 4 Roman Catholic organizations asked Mayor E. A. Bourque and the Board of Control for a bylaw forbidding the wearing of shorts in public except at beaches. A bylaw banning shorts was nothing new to the National Capital Region as both Aylmer and Hull already had bylaws banning shorts for years. A 40-year-old West End housewife spearheaded the campaign[1]. She argued:

I don't believe it is good for my children or anybody's children to see people walking around dressed like that.

“Sparking Shorts Ban,” Ottawa Citizen, June 14, 1950, 20.

The campaign picked up steam, with more religious institutions joining the fight from Quebec[2] while the Separate School Board forbade teachers to organize without the board’s permission after a group of teachers showed support for the ban[3].

On the other side of the debate was a coalition of Protestant churches saying they were against the ban as they saw it as a threat against the freedom of the individual[4].

The Mayor’s office received 1,902 letters, mostly form letters, from people who supported the ban while the Board of Control received 283 letters. There was also a heated exchange when the Mayor met with Mrs. Brault, the 40-year-old woman who started the campaign, on June 27. Brault said the mayor was nice but told the mayor his inaction might affect his re-election campaign. The city reported the mayor saying to Brault "You mind your business and I'll mind mine"[5]. The Mayor also referred to the woman as “very nasty”[6].

However, it was revealed by the City Solicitor, Gordon Medcalf, that the Municipal Act had no provision authorizing the city council to pass a by-law prohibiting shorts on the street[7]. With this, the Mayor and the Board of Control would not take any action and hoped the matter would soon be forgotten[6].

On March 28, 1951, a French-language Catholic Action group raised the issue again. They warned the new mayor, Grenville W. Goodwin, that the previous mayor lost votes due to his inaction. However, Goodwin once again reminded them of the City Solicitor’s ruling[8].
